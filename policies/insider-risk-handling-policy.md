# Insider Risk Handling Policy

Version 1.0 · Owner: Insider Risk Programme · Written by Group 8A for the capstone, informed by the NIST Privacy Framework and public insider-risk programme guidance. This is a fictional organisational policy, not legal advice.

## Purpose

This policy sets out how the Organisation reviews unusual file-access activity detected by user behaviour analytics. The goals are to protect sensitive information, to treat every person fairly and with dignity, and to make sure no action affecting a person is taken without human review.

## Scope

The policy covers automated analysis of file-access, removable-media and logon metadata on managed systems. It does not cover file contents, email, web browsing, keystrokes or any personal device. Analysis uses pseudonymous identifiers. Re-identifying a person needs Reviewer approval (see the Privacy and Fairness Standard).

## Guiding principles

1. **Presumption of good faith.** Unusual activity usually has a legitimate explanation: deadlines, new projects, covering for colleagues, on-call duties or tooling changes. A detection is a reason to look, not a conclusion.
2. **Proportionality.** Use the least intrusive response that resolves the question. Start by documenting and checking context before considering any restriction.
3. **Evidence over inference.** Case records state observed activity, its baseline and the policy that applies. They never speculate about motive, character, personal circumstances or protected characteristics.
4. **Human decision.** Automated systems rank and explain. People decide. Every recommendation that affects a person's access, employment or legal standing needs Reviewer approval.
5. **Confidentiality.** Case details go only to people with a role in the case.

## Roles

- **Analyst:** reviews ranked detections, verifies evidence, documents context and proposes next steps. An Analyst cannot authorise actions that affect a person.
- **Reviewer:** a senior analyst, privacy officer or HR partner who approves, rejects or requests more information on each case summary.
- **Line manager:** may be asked, through the Reviewer, to confirm whether activity matches assigned work. Managers aren't told that a detection exists unless the Reviewer approves it.
- **HR and Legal:** engaged only through an approved referral.

## Case lifecycle

1. **Detect:** analytics flag activity that deviates from the person's baseline and produce a ranked Finding with evidence.
2. **Triage:** the Analyst checks the evidence for data errors and obvious benign explanations, and records the outcome. Many cases close here.
3. **Summarise:** a case summary is prepared with observations, evidence, policy citations, possible benign explanations and recommended actions. Its status is *pending review*.
4. **Review:** the Reviewer approves, rejects or requests more information. Recommendations marked as needing approval have no effect until approved.
5. **Act or close:** approved actions are carried out and documented. Rejected cases are closed with a reason and kept, so false positives can be measured.

## Prohibited practices

- Taking any action that restricts access, refers a person to HR or Legal, or contacts the person, without Reviewer approval.
- Describing a person as malicious, guilty, a threat or an insider threat in any case record.
- Accessing file contents, personal accounts or personal devices as part of triage.
- Using protected characteristics, health, personality assessments or personal circumstances as risk indicators.
